When argan oil became famous, a lot of oil that isn't quite argan oil became famous with it. Some is diluted with cheaper oils. Some is culinary oil sold as cosmetic. Some is refined until most of what makes argan special is gone. And some bottles labelled "argan" contain mostly something else.

Whether you buy a single bottle or a 200-liter drum, you can protect yourself. Some checks take ten seconds in a shop; others need a lab. This guide explains both, and which home "tests" you shouldn't trust.

## Why Fake and Diluted Argan Oil Is So Common

Argan oil is slow and expensive to make. The argan tree grows almost only in southwestern Morocco, and it takes a large quantity of fruit - tens of kilograms - to produce one liter of oil. Cheaper oils such as sunflower or soybean cost a fraction of that price and look similar once mixed. That price gap is the whole reason adulteration exists.

## 1. Read the INCI List

This is the fastest and most reliable check you can do without a lab. Pure argan oil lists one ingredient: **Argania Spinosa Kernel Oil**.

- Other oils in the list (sunflower, soybean, jojoba) mean it's a blend.
- "Parfum" or "fragrance" means added scent.
- Silicones (anything ending in "-cone" or "-siloxane") mean it's a hair serum with some argan, not argan oil.

Blends aren't illegal - but they shouldn't be sold or priced as pure argan oil.

## 2. Check the Smell

- **Cosmetic argan oil:** light, slightly nutty, a little earthy. It fades quickly on the skin.
- **Culinary argan oil:** a strong toasted, nutty smell, because the kernels were roasted.
- **No smell at all:** possibly deodorized oil (a legitimate product for formulators), or refined or diluted oil.
- **Rancid, paint-like or fishy smell:** oxidized oil. Don't buy it, whatever the label says.

## 3. Look at the Color

Cosmetic argan oil is golden yellow. Culinary oil is darker amber. Very pale, almost clear oil deserves questions: it may be refined or diluted.

Color alone is not proof. It varies with the harvest, the pressing and filtration. Use it as a warning sign, not a verdict.

## 4. Feel the Texture

Rub a few drops between your fingers and onto the back of your hand. Pure argan oil feels silky and absorbs within a few minutes, leaving the skin soft rather than greasy. An oil that stays on the surface as a heavy film for a long time may be blended with a heavier oil.

## 5. Check the Packaging

Serious producers sell argan oil in dark glass - amber, cobalt or frosted - because light damages the oil. Clear plastic is a bad sign for a product meant to be kept for months. Also look for a batch number and an expiry date or period-after-opening symbol: missing batch numbers often mean missing quality control.

## 6. Compare the Price

Argan oil cannot be cheap. If a 100 ml bottle of "100% pure organic argan oil" costs less than a good olive oil, something is wrong. The same rule applies in bulk: offers far below what established producers quote for the same grade are the biggest single warning sign.

## 7. Ask for a COA

For any professional purchase, ask for the Certificate of Analysis of the batch you will receive. A useful COA shows:

- a batch number, analysis date and lab name,
- acidity (a freshness indicator),
- peroxide value (oxidation),
- the fatty acid profile,
- ideally, the sterol profile.

A supplier who sends the same PDF for every order, or no COA at all, is telling you something. Our guide on how to choose an argan oil supplier explains how to read each value.

## 8. Use a Lab Test for Bulk Purchases

For bulk orders, a laboratory analysis is the only reliable way to detect dilution. Two analyses matter most:

- **Fatty acid profile:** argan oil has a characteristic balance of oleic and linoleic acids. Large deviations suggest another oil.
- **Sterol profile:** argan oil's sterols are unusual - schottenol and spinasterol dominate, and campesterol is present only at very low levels. Many cheaper oils contain much more campesterol, so mixing usually shows up clearly.

The cost of a lab panel is small compared with one contaminated drum. Test the approved sample, then test the first production batch.

## Home "Tests" You Shouldn't Rely On

You'll find many tricks online. Most are unreliable:

- **The fridge test:** the idea that pure argan oil turns cloudy or solid in the fridge. Many oils react to cold, and blends can behave the same way. Not conclusive.
- **The paper test:** "pure oil leaves no mark on paper". All oils leave a mark.
- **The "vanishing" test:** absorption speed depends on your skin, the amount and the room temperature.
- **Color as proof of mixing:** color varies naturally, as explained above.

Use the INCI list, smell, packaging, price and documents for everyday decisions. Use a lab for professional ones.

## Cosmetic vs. Culinary Argan Oil

The two are made from the same kernels, but culinary oil comes from roasted kernels and is made for food. They are different products, with different rules and prices. Culinary oil sold as cosmetic, or the reverse, is a common form of mislabeling. Ask which grade you're buying, and check the smell.

## If You Suspect Your Oil Is Fake

- **As a consumer:** stop using it if it smells rancid, contact the seller, and report the listing to the marketplace if the label is misleading.
- **As a business buyer:** quarantine the batch, send a sample to an independent lab, compare it with your retained approved sample, and document everything before contacting the supplier. A clear written specification in your order makes this conversation much easier.

## FAQ

### What does pure argan oil smell like?

Cosmetic argan oil has a light, slightly nutty scent that fades quickly. A strong toasted smell means culinary oil; a rancid smell means the oil has oxidized.

### Can I test argan oil at home?

You can check the INCI list, smell, color, texture and packaging. These catch obvious problems, but only a lab analysis of fatty acids and sterols can confirm purity.

### Is deodorized argan oil fake?

No. Deodorized argan oil is a legitimate product preferred by some formulators. It should be labelled and sold as deodorized, not as virgin oil with its natural scent.

### Does pure argan oil solidify in the fridge?

It can turn cloudy in the cold, but so can many other oils and blends. The fridge test does not prove purity.
